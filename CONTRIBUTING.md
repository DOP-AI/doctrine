# Contributing to DOP-AI

## Dignity Over Power

DOP-AI exists to build systems that protect people before collapse occurs.

All contributions must align with the foundational doctrine:

- dignity over power
- prevention over reaction
- transparency over opacity
- resilience over fragility
- human-readable oversight
- fail-soft architecture

---

# Core Contribution Principles

## Human Impact First

Technology is not the mission.

Human stability, safety, resilience, and dignity are the mission.

All systems must be evaluated based on:
- public safety
- systemic stability
- transparency
- operational clarity
- long-term resilience

---

## No Silent Failures

Systems must never fail invisibly.

Contributors should prioritize:
- observable state
- explainable alerts
- transparent escalation
- operational traceability

---

## Explainability Matters

All operational intelligence systems should remain understandable by humans.

Contributors should avoid:
- unnecessary opacity
- hidden logic
- deceptive automation
- manipulative system behavior

---

# Repository Standards

## Documentation First

Major architectural changes should include:
- diagrams
- documentation
- operational reasoning
- system impact analysis

---

## Modular Architecture

Contributors should favor:
- composable systems
- modular infrastructure
- isolated failure domains
- scalable telemetry pipelines

---

## Security and Safety

Contributors must avoid:
- malicious code
- surveillance abuse
- exploitative automation
- unsafe autonomous escalation

---

# Collaboration Standards

## Respectful Participation

DOP-AI welcomes:
- engineers
- researchers
- civic technologists
- environmental analysts
- governance thinkers
- public-interest contributors

Harassment, abuse, and bad-faith participation are not tolerated.

---

# Mission

To build transparent, resilient, dignity-first operational intelligence systems that strengthen public trust and reduce systemic harm.

---

# Doctrine Alignment

All repositories under DOP-AI are expected to align with the foundational doctrine and operational principles defined in:

- doctrine

Build systems that continue protecting people even when institutions fail.
